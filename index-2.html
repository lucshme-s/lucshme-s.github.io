Profile

Summary of practice

My work sits at the point where a machine meets the floor it has to survive on. At PepsiCo's Quaker Oats plant I work on capital and productivity projects on packaging and oven lines — automating a manual checkweigher inspection, supporting guarding design and installation on production ovens with the millwrights who maintain them, decoupling an oven to remove a throughput constraint, and replacing a conveyance system that was damaging product.

Before that I modelled and simulated building envelope assemblies in SolidWorks and AutoCAD, ran FEA against load cases, and wrote the code-compliance reports and general arrangement drawings that went with them. Earlier still, at General Motors, I led a team of 50+ unionised skilled trades, diagnosed fluid and electromechanical systems in a high-volume manufacturing plant, and authored the SAP specifications, SOPs and compliance records behind them.

The consistent thread is a bias toward the whole lifecycle: define the requirement with the people who asked for it, lay it out in 3D, design to the standard that applies, be present through build and commissioning, and leave behind documentation good enough that the machine keeps performing after I move on.
